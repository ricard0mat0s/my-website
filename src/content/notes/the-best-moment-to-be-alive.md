I feel lucky to be alive at a time when AI makes it easier to learn and build. I can get immediate feedback, discuss an approach when I'm stuck, and find resources to study. I use AI as a pair-programming guide: it helps me move forward, but I still have to decide what makes sense for the work.

Recently, I've been building [personal-memory](/projects/personal-memory/), a project to organize information from my projects and my life. While working on authentication for its MCP server, I wrote tests and asked AI focused questions, including which library to use to create a process and how to write the body of an MCP request. I applied its suggestions, then checked whether my tests covered the behavior I wanted.

AI doesn't always give me a perfect answer, and an early prototype doesn't need to be perfect to help me learn. Critical thinking and creativity still matter. For personal-memory, I chose a workflow where I review proposed changes before the app applies them to my files. AI can help me explore how to build that workflow, but deciding that it matters is my responsibility.

I'm excited by how much more I can build now. If you're a new developer, pick a small idea and build a first version with AI's help. In my next post, I'll explore another choice this technology gives us: how much of the work we want to delegate to it.
